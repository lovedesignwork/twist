The easiest way to choose a cocktail in Phuket Old Town is to describe **the flavours you enjoy, how sweet you like your drinks, and any ingredients you avoid**. You do not need a favourite spirit or an encyclopaedic knowledge of classic recipes. A sentence such as “something refreshing, citrusy and not very sweet” gives the bar team a useful starting point.

At TWIST, cocktails are part of an evening that can also include dinner and the view. This guide helps you order with confidence without assigning unverified ingredients to signature drinks or assuming that every colourful glass tastes fruity.

## Begin with flavour rather than appearance

A drink's colour is a poor guide to how it tastes. A vivid cocktail may be tart or bitter; a clear drink may be sweet or strong. Glass shape, garnish and foam can suggest a style, but they do not replace asking what is in the recipe.

Think about drinks you already enjoy, including non-alcoholic ones. If you like sparkling water with lime, you may enjoy a crisp, citrus-led direction. If you prefer black coffee or tonic, bitterness may appeal. If fruit juice is usually your choice, ask for fruit character while explaining how much sweetness you want.

Use two or three descriptors, not a long list of competing instructions. “Fruity, tart and lightly sweet” is clearer than asking for something sweet, sour, bitter, strong, light and refreshing all at once. The team can then explain which current options come closest.

## Understand four useful flavour directions

| Direction | What to ask about | Useful phrase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Citrus-led | Tartness, sweetness and length | Bright and refreshing, not too sweet |
| Fruit-led | Actual fruit ingredients and balance | Fruity, with some acidity |
| Bitter or herbal | Bitter ingredients and intensity | Aromatic, with a dry finish |
| Spirit-forward | Base spirit, dilution and strength | A slow-sipping drink with clear spirit character |

These are ordering categories, not rigid rules. One cocktail can sit between several of them. A bitter drink may contain sugar; a fruit-led drink may still be strong. Ask for the recipe's main ingredients if you want a clearer picture.

Temperature and texture matter too. A long drink with ice and bubbles feels different from a small chilled serve, even when both use similar citrus or spirits. Think about whether you want something to sip through dinner or a short drink before ordering food.

## Treat signature names as a conversation starter

TWIST's [official venue information](https://royalphuketcity.com/twist-rooftop-restaurant-bar) lists signature names including Rosy Nyonya, Phuket Cooler, Butik Twist, 3BS, Anda Dibuk and Drunken Miner. Those names tell you what to ask about; they do not establish the recipes, availability or serving style on your visit.

Use the current cocktail menu linked from the [homepage](/), then ask the team to explain a drink that interests you. Confirm the base spirit, whether it is sweet or dry, and any ingredients you need to avoid. Do not assume that a name referencing Phuket means every ingredient is locally grown.

A useful request might be: “I am interested in this signature, but I prefer a tart drink. Is it a good fit?” That gives the bartender room to recommend an existing choice rather than promise a custom version that may not suit the recipe.

## Be specific about sweetness and strength

“Not too sweet” means different things to different people. If possible, compare it with something familiar: less sweet than a fruit juice, drier than your usual spritz, or with enough sweetness to soften sharp citrus. Ask which menu option is closest before requesting changes.

Sweetness also does not tell you how much alcohol a drink contains. A smooth or fruity cocktail can still be spirit-heavy. If you prefer a lighter option, say so directly and ask what the team can offer. Do not rely on the size of the glass as a measure of strength.

There is no need to match the pace of the table. Choose a drink you want, have water alongside it and decide independently whether to order another. If you are driving, choose a non-alcoholic option and arrange the evening accordingly.

## Match the drink to the meal you actually want

Start with the food rather than an abstract pairing rule. If you are ordering several different dishes, an easygoing drink you enjoy may be more useful than trying to find one perfect match for every flavour. Water remains a good companion throughout the meal.

A richer dish may make you want a refreshing contrast; a strongly seasoned dish may make a very delicate cocktail harder to notice. Treat those as preferences to explore, not universal rules. Ask the team what works with the dishes you have chosen from the [TWIST menu](/menu).

If you plan a full dinner, avoid ordering all your drinks before deciding on food. The menu, appetite and pace of service can help shape the next round. For couples with different preferences, two different drinks often make more sense than a matching pair chosen for the photograph.

## Mention ingredients you need to avoid

Tell the team about allergies and dietary requirements before ordering. Ingredients can appear in syrups, foams, bitters, garnishes and premixed components, not only in the headline description. Asking whether a drink contains a specific ingredient is more reliable than judging it by appearance.

For alcohol-free choices, ask what is available and clarify your needs. Some cocktail ingredients can contain alcohol even when the main spirit is omitted. Let the team explain the preparation rather than assuming any cocktail can become an equivalent alcohol-free drink.

The same approach applies to caffeine or other ingredients you prefer not to have late in the evening. A clear question is easier to answer than a broad request for something “healthy” or “light.”

## Quick questions

### Should I choose a signature cocktail or a classic?

Choose according to flavour and curiosity. A signature can introduce something unfamiliar; a classic gives you a reference point. Ask about the actual recipe and current availability in either case.

### Can I ask for less sweetness?

Yes, but some components may already be prepared. Ask whether the recipe can be adjusted or whether another menu choice would suit you better.

### Can I visit without drinking alcohol?

You can plan an evening around food and the view and ask about current non-alcoholic choices. [Request a table](/reserve), decide whether you are staying for dinner, and let your own preferences set the pace.
